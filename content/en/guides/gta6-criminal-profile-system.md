The most interesting thing to come out of the September previews isn't a new map or a new car — it's a system that stops asking "are you good or evil" and starts asking "what kind of crook are you."

Everything below is **reported** from creator previews and press coverage, not a formal Rockstar announcement. Treat it as "widely reported, pending final confirmation."

## How it's different from RDR2's Honor

RDR2's Honor was a good/evil scale that nudged you toward being nice. GTA6's reported Criminal Profile doesn't care if you're nice. It tracks your **style**:

- **Professional** — clean, controlled, surgical. You plan the score and get out clean.
- **Aggressive** — you push, you escalate, but you're not unhinged.
- **Violent** — blood first, questions later.
- **Psycho** — you're the reason the news says "unprecedented."

Your actions move you between these states over time. It's not one mission's rating — it's the accumulated shape of how you play.

## The point of no return

The detail people keep quoting: if you go far enough off the rails, the profile icon **shatters and locks permanently**. From then on the game stops grading you entirely.

That's the part that matters. The system isn't a leash — it's a mirror. It watches you until you're too far gone to grade, then it shrugs and lets you be.

## Each protagonist gets their own

Jason and Lucia each carry a **separate** profile. So you can play Jason as a clean Professional and Lucia as a full Psycho, and the game treats them as different people — which they are.

That's a genuine first for the series: two leads, two criminal reputations, one save file.

## The self-defense nuance

Reports say the system judges *reasonableness*, not just results. Defending yourself in a fight doesn't ding you the way an unprovoked rampage does. That's a big departure from GTA5, where the game mostly didn't judge you at all.

## What it means for how you play

If this lands as reported, GTA6 has a soft role-playing layer built in:
- You can commit to a "style" and let the world react.
- Or you can turn the notifications off and ignore it completely.

Either way, it's Rockstar finally giving GTA a memory of who you've been — without turning it into a morality lecture.
