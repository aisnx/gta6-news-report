We finally have a clear answer on GTA 6's driving: it's not GTA 4's "heavy to the point of frustrating," and it's not GTA 5's "light and floaty." It's both, split down the middle, and the physics were rebuilt from scratch.

## Who said it

Rob Nelson, co-studio head of Rockstar North, in an interview at September's preview event (via TGG, relayed by GamesRadar, IGN, and others). That's first-party, from a developer, not a leak.

## What "the best of both" actually means

Nelson's point: asked whether the cars would feel "heavy" like GTA 4, he said yes, but only "in the best way possible." In plain terms:

- You can clearly feel the car's weight, instead of GTA 5's toy-car feel.
- But it's not sluggish. It stays as approachable and pleasant to drive as GTA 5.

The short version: it keeps the part of GTA 4 people miss the most (the weight, the feel), without losing GTA 5's ease.

## Rebuilt from scratch

The physics were redone for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Nelson says that gave the team far more control, letting them tune:

- Drivetrain
- Tires
- Suspension
- Steering

## One concrete result: shot tires send you rolling

It was already visible in August's Extended Look: shoot out a tire and the car can slide, spin, and roll several times. That's a lot more dramatic than GTA 5, where a flat tire just makes you drift a little.

The cars also reportedly hit higher top speeds than GTA 5, with a more convincing sense of speed.

## There's a race track too

GTA 6 adds a proper circuit near Port Gellhorn, with laps, timing, and live position tracking, so you can run real races.

## What it means

GTA 6's driving isn't a reskin of GTA 5. It's trying to please two camps at once: players who miss GTA 4's feel, and players who found GTA 4 too heavy. If the balance lands, it could be the best-driving GTA yet.
