Before the official demo even aired, real gameplay clips — Jason, Lucia, car chases, story beats — leaked online. Now a group calling itself **CyberLeek** has claimed responsibility, Take-Two is going to court to find the source, and Rockstar is publicly unhappy. Here's the timeline.

## What leaked

- Unauthorized footage from a playable build: Jason, Lucia, driving, and story-related content.
- It circulated in the days right before Rockstar's own *An Extended Look* premiered.

## Who's claiming it

- A group called **CyberLeek** says it's responsible.
- Part of the group's activity, per reports, is tied to promoting a digital currency — a red flag all by itself.

## The legal response

- Take-Two is taking legal steps to force **Microsoft and Discord** to hand over data that could identify the leakers.
- It's the same playbook the industry used for earlier high-profile leaks: go after the platforms, not just the leakers.

## Rockstar's reaction

Rockstar called the leak **"heartbreaking"** for the team, but stressed the game is **"nearly complete"** and still on track for November 19.

In other words: the leak hurt morale, it didn't hurt the schedule.

## The bottom line

None of the leaked footage is official, and this site won't repost it. The one real takeaway is the timing — Rockstar chose to respond to a leak by going public with confidence about the release date, which is about as reassuring as it gets.
